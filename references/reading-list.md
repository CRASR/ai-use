A curated list of readings behind CRASR's [AI-use policy](https://github.com/CRASR/ai-use) and [how we use AI](https://crasr.github.io/ai.html). It is not a systematic review. Each entry has a one-line note on what kind of evidence it is and why it is here. Preprints, blog posts, and documentation are marked; they have not been peer reviewed.

Download the full list: [RIS](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CRASR/ai-use/main/references/ai-science-reading-list.ris) (Zotero, EndNote, Mendeley) · [BibTeX](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CRASR/ai-use/main/references/ai-science-reading-list.bib)

## Incentives and the science of science

- [Bergstrom and West (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02411-7). How publishers can fight misinformation in and about science and medicine. *Nature Medicine*.  
  Commentary anticipating generative AI flooding review and raising verification burdens.
- [Gartenberg et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1287/orsc.2026.ed.v37.n3). More versus better: Artificial intelligence, incentives, and the emerging crisis in peer review. *Organization Science*.  
  Editorial with journal-level data: AI-era submissions rose while writing quality fell, adding review burden without matching gains.
- [Hao et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09922-y). Artificial intelligence tools expand scientists' impact but contract science's focus. *Nature* 649: 1237-1243.  
  Bibliometric study of 41 million papers: AI use tracks with individual career gains and a narrower collective scientific focus.
- [Ioannidis (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.0020124). Why most published research findings are false. *PLOS Medicine* 2(8): e124.  
  Formal analysis of how power, bias, and selection shape the reliability of published findings.
- [Munafò et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-016-0021). A manifesto for reproducible science. *Nature Human Behaviour* 1: 0021.  
  Practices, incentives, and institutional reform for reproducibility; better tools need better incentives.
- [Noy and Zhang (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adh2586). Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence. *Science* 381: 187-192.  
  Randomized experiment: time and quality gains on professional writing tasks. A counterweight to a pure speed-versus-quality story.
- [Smaldino and McElreath (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.160384). The natural selection of bad science. *Royal Society Open Science* 3: 160384.  
  Formal model: rewarding publication counts selects for weak methods without anyone intending misconduct.
- [Wang et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06221-2). Scientific discovery in the age of artificial intelligence. *Nature* 620: 47-60.  
  Review of AI's contributions across scientific discovery, broader than language-model assistants.
- [West and Bergstrom (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1912444117). Misinformation in and about science. *PNAS* 118(15): e1912444117.  
  Synthesis linking misinformation within science to incentives and publishing practice. See the 2024 correction.

## Understanding, judgment, and learning

- [Amano et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.3002184). The manifold costs of being a non-native English speaker in science. *PLOS Biology* 21(7): e3002184.  
  Survey of environmental scientists: non-native English speakers spend up to twice as long reading and writing papers.
- [Bastani et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122). Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. *PNAS* 122: e2422633122.  
  Field experiment separating assisted performance from learning; tutor design matters.
- [Berdejo-Espinola and Amano (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adg9714). AI tools can improve equity in science. *Science* 379: 991.  
  Letter arguing AI translation and editing can reduce language barriers in science.
- [Emig (1977)](https://doi.org/10.58680/ccc197716382). Writing as a mode of learning. *College Composition and Communication* 28(2): 122-128.  
  Foundational argument that writing is a way of learning, not only of reporting finished ideas.
- [Goddard et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1136/amiajnl-2011-000089). Automation bias: A systematic review of frequency, effect mediators, and mitigators. *Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association* 19(1): 121-127.  
  Systematic review of over-reliance on automated recommendations; predates language models.
- [Graham et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654320914744). The effects of writing on learning in science, social studies, and mathematics: A meta-analysis. *Review of Educational Research* 90(2): 179-226.  
  Meta-analysis: writing supports learning in school settings.
- [Kazemitabaar et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642773). CodeAid: Evaluating a classroom deployment of an LLM-based programming assistant that balances student and educator needs. *Proceedings of the 2024 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems*.  
  A semester-long classroom deployment of an assistant built to explain rather than hand over code.
- [Kosmyna et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2506.08872). Your brain on ChatGPT: Accumulation of cognitive debt when using an AI assistant for essay writing task. *arXiv:2506.08872*. [Preprint]  
  EEG study: essay writers using an AI assistant showed weaker brain connectivity, lower ownership, and poorer recall of their own text.
- [Lee et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3706598.3713778). The impact of generative AI on critical thinking: Self-reported reductions in cognitive effort and confidence effects from a survey of knowledge workers. *Proceedings of the 2025 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems*.  
  Survey: more confidence in AI went with less reported critical thinking (self-report, not causal).
- [Messeri and Crockett (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07146-0). Artificial intelligence and illusions of understanding in scientific research. *Nature* 627: 49-58.  
  Conceptual account of how AI can raise output while narrowing understanding and intellectual diversity.
- [Nature Reviews Bioengineering (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44222-025-00323-4). Writing is thinking. *Nature Reviews Bioengineering* 3: 431.  
  Editorial on the value of human-generated scientific writing in the age of language models.
- [Sharma et al. (2024)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/0105f7972202c1d4fb817da9f21a9663-Abstract-Conference.html). Towards understanding sycophancy in language models. *International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2024)*.  
  Experiments showing assistants tend to agree with users even against the truth; AI approval is not independent criticism.
- [Shen and Tamkin (2026)](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2601.20245). How AI impacts skill formation. *arXiv:2601.20245*. [Preprint]  
  Randomized trial: developers who delegated a new task to AI learned it less well than those who asked conceptual questions.
- [Çetinkaya-Rundel (2026)](https://speakerdeck.com/minecr/beyond-the-prompt-rai). Beyond the prompt: Programming as a pathway to statistical thinking. *Talk, R+AI Conference*. [Slides]  
  Teaching data science with AI built into coursework. The [talk materials](https://github.com/mine-cetinkaya-rundel/beyond-the-prompt-rai-2026) include a student [`AGENTS.md`](https://github.com/mine-cetinkaya-rundel/beyond-the-prompt-rai-2026/blob/main/examples/hw/AGENTS.md) that tutors rather than solves.

## Evidence and inquiry: philosophical foundations

- [Bergstrom and West (2020)](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/563882/calling-bullshit-by-carl-t-bergstrom-and-jevin-d-west/9780525509196/). Calling Bullshit: The Art of Skepticism in a Data-Driven World. *Random House*.  
  Practical skepticism about quantitative claims, selection effects, and causal interpretation.
- Dewey (1938). Logic: The Theory of Inquiry. *Henry Holt*.  
  Source of 'warranted assertibility': conclusions are justified by the inquiry that supports them.
- [Frankfurt (2005)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7t4wr). On Bullshit. *Princeton University Press*.  
  Distinguishes indifference to truth from lying; fluent output does not supply warrant (essay first published 1986).
- [Haack (2003)](https://repository.law.miami.edu/fac_books/146/). Defending Science—Within Reason: Between Scientism and Cynicism. *Prometheus Books*.  
  Fallibilist assessment of evidence, between scientism and cynicism.
- [Hicks et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10676-024-09775-5). ChatGPT is bullshit. *Ethics and Information Technology* 26: 38.  
  Applies Frankfurt to language-model output; a contested philosophical argument, not an error rate.
- [Longino (1990)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctvx5wbfz). Science as Social Knowledge: Values and Objectivity in Scientific Inquiry. *Princeton University Press*.  
  Objectivity through social criticism; an agreeable assistant cannot replace a critical community.
- [McIntyre (2019)](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/12203.001.0001). The Scientific Attitude: Defending Science from Denial, Fraud, and Pseudoscience. *MIT Press*.  
  Science grounded in caring about evidence and being willing to change one's mind.

## Principles and guidance for responsible use

- [Blau et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2407886121). Protecting scientific integrity in an age of generative AI. *PNAS* 121(22): e2407886121.  
  Editorial from an NAS-convened panel: accountability, disclosure, verification, documentation, and oversight.
- [Castellacci et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2607.24879). Generative artificial intelligence in scientific research: Individual benefits, collective risks, and a framework for responsible research with AI. *arXiv:2607.24879*. [Preprint]  
  Framework weighing individual gains against collective risks to science.
- [Committee on Publication Ethics (2023)](https://publicationethics.org/cope-position-statements/ai-author). Authorship and AI tools: COPE position statement.  
  AI tools cannot be authors; authors are responsible for all content and must disclose use.
- [European Commission, ERA Forum (2026)](https://research-and-innovation.ec.europa.eu/news/all-research-and-innovation-news/updated-era-living-guidelines-responsible-use-generative-ai-research-2026-05-08_en). Living guidelines on the responsible use of generative AI in research (third version). *European Commission*.  
  Living guidance for researchers, institutions, and funders in the European Research Area.
- [Hosseini and Horbach (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41073-023-00133-5). Fighting reviewer fatigue or amplifying bias? Considerations and recommendations for use of ChatGPT and other large language models in scholarly peer review. *Research Integrity and Peer Review* 8: 4.  
  Peer review as a human, community judgment, and what language models would change about it.
- [International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (2026)](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/artificial-intelligence/). Recommendations, Section V: Use of artificial intelligence in publishing.  
  Publisher-side expectations for disclosure, placement, and responsibility (updated January 2026).
- [Knöchel et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-025-00768-8). Core principles of responsible generative AI usage in research. *AI and Ethics* 5: 6371-6377.  
  Consensus principles, including data protection and when documentation of AI use is needed.
- [National Institutes of Health (2023)](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-23-149.html). The use of generative artificial intelligence technologies is prohibited for the NIH peer review process (NOT-OD-23-149). *NIH*.  
  Funder prohibition on AI in grant review, grounded in confidentiality.
- [National Science Foundation (2023)](https://www.nsf.gov/news/notice-to-the-research-community-on-ai). Notice to research community: Use of generative artificial intelligence technology in the NSF merit review process. *NSF*.  
  Sharing proposal content with AI tools breaches merit-review confidentiality.
- [Resnik and Hosseini (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-024-00493-8). The ethics of using artificial intelligence in scientific research: New guidance needed for a new tool. *AI and Ethics*.  
  Accountability, transparency, fairness, and reproducibility as research-ethics questions.
- [UNESCO (2021)](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-open-science). Recommendation on Open Science. *UNESCO*.  
  International instrument making equity, infrastructure, and access integral to open science.
- [UNESCO (2023)](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/guidance-generative-ai-education-and-research). Guidance for generative AI in education and research. *UNESCO*.  
  Human-centred guidance on learning, agency, inclusion, and governance.
- [van Dis et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/d41586-023-00288-7). ChatGPT: Five priorities for research. *Nature* 614: 224-226.  
  Early agenda for governing language-model use in research.

## Disclosing and documenting AI use

- [Cwik (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41073-026-00245-8). Disclosure is not documentation: An open science framework for documenting generative AI use in scholarly research and publication workflows. *Research Integrity and Peer Review* 11: 47.  
  Task-specific documentation with reusable templates; the closest framework to CRASR's ai-use log.
- [Hosseini et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1177/17470161251345499). Disclosing generative AI use for writing assistance should be voluntary. *Research Ethics*.  
  Argues writing assistance need not be disclosed; contrast with mandatory-disclosure positions.
- [Hosseini et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1177/17470161231180449). The ethics of disclosing the use of artificial intelligence tools in writing scholarly manuscripts. *Research Ethics*.  
  Early ethical case for disclosing AI use in manuscripts.
- [Luo et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjebm-2025-113825). Reporting guideline for the use of generative artificial intelligence tools in medical research: The GAMER statement. *BMJ Evidence-Based Medicine*.  
  Delphi reporting guideline: tool, purpose, prompts, and verification.
- [Resnik and Hosseini (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1080/08989621.2025.2481949). Disclosing artificial intelligence use in scientific research and publication: When should disclosure be mandatory, optional, or unnecessary?. *Accountability in Research*.  
  Ties the need for disclosure to what the AI did, the basis for CRASR's risk tiers.
- [STM Association (2025)](https://stm-assoc.org/document/recommendations-for-a-classification-of-ai-use-in-academic-manuscript-preparation/). Recommendations for a classification of AI use in academic manuscript preparation. *STM*.  
  Publisher classification of AI uses and which need disclosure.
- [Suchikova et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1080/08989621.2025.2544331). GAIDeT (Generative AI Delegation Taxonomy): A taxonomy for humans to delegate tasks to generative artificial intelligence in scientific research and publishing. *Accountability in Research*.  
  Taxonomy of tasks delegated to AI, with a declaration generator.
- [The Scholarly Kitchen (2026)](https://scholarlykitchen.sspnet.org/2026/07/13/guest-post-research-needs-a-shared-standard-for-ai-disclosure/). Guest post: Research needs a shared standard for AI disclosure.  
  On the World Conferences on Research Integrity effort toward one disclosure standard.
- [Wahle et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1109/JCDL57899.2023.00060). AI Usage Cards: Responsibly reporting AI-generated content. *2023 ACM/IEEE Joint Conference on Digital Libraries (JCDL)*: 282-284.  
  Structured, machine-readable reporting of AI use, including how output was checked.
- [Weaver (2024)](https://doi.org/10.5860/crln.85.10.407). The Artificial Intelligence Disclosure (AID) Framework: An introduction. *College & Research Libraries News* 85(10): 407-411.  
  Stage-by-stage disclosure statement format; the source of CRASR's log stages.
- [World Bank (2026)](https://worldbank.github.io/wb-reproducible-research-repository/guidance/AI_reproducibility_guidelines.html). Documenting AI use for reproducible research. *World Bank Reproducible Research Repository*.  
  Separates AI as coding help from AI outputs used as data; what to keep for each.

## Scientific workflow, statistics, and reproducibility

- [Gelman et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2011.01808). Bayesian workflow. *arXiv:2011.01808*. [Preprint]  
  Iterative model building, computational and model checking, comparison, and understanding.
- [Kapoor and Narayanan (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804). Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science. *Patterns* 4(9): 100804.  
  Data leakage across many fields; check validation design before trusting performance.
- [Kapoor et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adk3452). REFORMS: Consensus-based recommendations for machine-learning-based science. *Science Advances* 10: eadk3452.  
  Consensus checklist for conducting and reporting ML-based research.
- [Nosek et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aab2374). Promoting an open research culture. *Science* 348: 1422-1425.  
  TOP guidelines: graded, implementable transparency standards for journals.
- [Shmueli (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1214/10-STS330). To explain or to predict?. *Statistical Science* 25(3): 289-310.  
  Explanatory and predictive goals call for different models and validation; decide which before modeling.
- [Smith et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011767). Ten simple rules for using large language models in science, version 1.0. *PLOS Computational Biology* 20(1): e1011767.  
  Practical rules for researchers using language models.

## AI in ecology and environmental science

- [Cooper et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.14325). Harnessing large language models for coding, teaching and inclusion to empower research in ecology and evolution. *Methods in Ecology and Evolution* 15(10): 1757-1763.  
  Perspective on coding, teaching, and inclusion, with a road map for responsible use.
- [Ferrari et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.74118). Thinking outside the black box: Seven guidelines for GenAI use in the ecological sciences. *Ecology and Evolution* 16(8): e74118.  
  Seven guidelines tying AI use to ecological theory, expertise, transparency, and equity.
- [Johnson et al. (2024)](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/218025). Pressure to publish introduces large-language model risks. *Methods in Ecology and Evolution* 15(10): 1771-1773.  
  Response to Cooper et al.: publication pressure turns language-model shortcuts into risks to quality and training.
- [King et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1014627). Ten simple rules for effective use of generative AI for code development in environmental science. *PLOS Computational Biology* 22(8): e1014627.  
  Rules for AI-assisted coding written for environmental scientists.
- [Mammides and Papadopoulos (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.14398). The role of large language models in interdisciplinary research: Opportunities, challenges and ways forward. *Methods in Ecology and Evolution* 15(10): 1774-1776.  
  Language models as bridges between fields, and the risks for complex tasks.

## AI-generated code: pitfalls and practice

- [Ahmad (2026)](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2604.13277). Comprehension debt in GenAI-assisted software engineering projects. *30th International Conference on Evaluation and Assessment in Software Engineering (EASE 2026)*.  
  Defines comprehension debt: the gap between the code a team has and the code it understands.
- [Anthropic (2026)](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices). Best practices for Claude Code. *Claude Code documentation*. [Documentation]  
  Vendor guidance: give the assistant a check to run, and enforce hard rules with hooks rather than instructions.
- [Anthropic (2026)](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/output-styles). Output styles. *Claude Code documentation*. [Documentation]  
  Includes a Learning style that leaves key pieces of code for the person to write.
- [Becker et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2507.09089). Measuring the impact of early-2025 AI on experienced open-source developer productivity. *arXiv:2507.09089*. [Preprint]  
  Randomized trial: experienced developers believed AI sped them up but were 19% slower.
- [Fawzy et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3786583.3786866). Vibe coding in practice: Motivations, challenges, and a future outlook – a grey literature review. *Proceedings of the IEEE/ACM 48th International Conference on Software Engineering: Software Engineering in Practice (ICSE-SEIP 2026)*: 212-223.  
  Defines vibe coding and reviews what practitioners report about it.
- [He et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3793302.3793349). Speed at the cost of quality: How Cursor AI increases short-term velocity and long-term complexity in open-source projects. *Proceedings of the 23rd International Conference on Mining Software Repositories (MSR 2026)*: 181-193.  
  Projects adopting an AI coding agent sped up briefly, then slowed as complexity and warnings rose.
- [Liu et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2603.28592). Debt behind the AI boom: A large-scale empirical study of AI-generated code in the wild. *arXiv:2603.28592*. [Preprint]  
  About 300,000 AI-written commits: the technical debt they introduced, mostly code smells, often persisted.
- [Merz (2026)](https://evanxmerz.com/post/ai-usage-generates-a-fragile-codebase/). AI usage generates a fragile codebase. [Blog post]  
  Practitioner essay on bloated helpers, shallow tests, and skimmed reviews.

## Environmental costs and equity

- [Bender et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3442188.3445922). On the dangers of stochastic parrots: Can language models be too big?. *Proceedings of the 2021 ACM Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency*.  
  Scale, training data, environmental cost, and unequal harms.
- [Couch (2026)](https://simonpcouch.com/blog/2026-01-20-cc-impact/). Electricity use of AI coding agents. [Blog post]  
  Rough estimate that a coding-agent session uses far more electricity than a chatbot query, though daily use stays small next to household energy.
- [Elsworth et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2508.15734). Measuring the environmental impact of delivering AI at Google scale. *arXiv:2508.15734*. [Preprint]  
  Google's own measurement of energy, emissions, and water per Gemini text prompt.
- [Hausfather (2026)](https://www.theclimatebrink.com/p/the-real-energy-use-of-agentic-ai). The real energy use of agentic AI. *The Climate Brink*. [Blog post]  
  Eight weeks of logged coding-agent use: most energy goes to re-reading context, far above per-chat figures.
- [Houston Advanced Research Center (2026)](https://harcresearch.org/news/texas-data-center-boom-could-consume-up-to-161-billion-gallons-of-water-annually-by-2030/). Thirsty data and the Lone Star State: The impact of data center growth on Texas' water supply. *HARC*.  
  Texas data-center water use now and projected to 2030.
- [International Energy Agency (2025)](https://www.iea.org/reports/energy-and-ai). Energy and AI. *IEA*.  
  Projections of data-center and AI electricity demand to 2030 and how it might be supplied.
- [Jegham et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2505.09598). How hungry is AI? Benchmarking energy, water, and carbon footprint of LLM inference. *arXiv:2505.09598*. [Preprint]  
  Per-prompt energy, water, and carbon estimates across commercial models.
- [Li et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3724499). Making AI less 'thirsty': Uncovering and addressing the secret water footprint of AI models. *Communications of the ACM* 68(7): 54-61.  
  Water footprint of AI, counting on-site cooling and electricity generation.
- [Luccioni et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3630106.3658542). Power hungry processing: Watts driving the cost of AI deployment?. *Proceedings of the 2024 ACM Conference on Fairness, Accountability, and Transparency*.  
  Measured inference energy across tasks and models.
- [Mistral AI (2025)](https://mistral.ai/news/our-contribution-to-a-global-environmental-standard-for-ai). Our contribution to a global environmental standard for AI. *Mistral AI*.  
  Externally reviewed lifecycle analysis of a large model, covering training and per-response emissions, water, and materials.
- [Schwartz et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3381831). Green AI. *Communications of the ACM* 63(12): 54-63.  
  Efficiency as a research goal alongside accuracy.
- [Shehabi et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.71468/P1WC7Q). 2024 United States data center energy usage report. *Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (LBNL-2001637)*.  
  US data centers used 4.4% of electricity in 2023, projected to reach 6.7-12% by 2028.
- [Strubell et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.18653/v1/P19-1355). Energy and policy considerations for deep learning in NLP. *Proceedings of the 57th Annual Meeting of the ACL*.  
  Foundational estimates of training costs and access barriers; not current per-prompt figures.
- [You (2025)](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/how-much-energy-does-chatgpt-use). How much energy does ChatGPT use? *Epoch AI*. [Blog post]  
  Estimates about 0.3 Wh for a typical chatbot query, a tenth of the often-cited 3 Wh.
